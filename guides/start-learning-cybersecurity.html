How to start learning cybersecurity as a complete beginner

Cybersecurity is the work of protecting systems, networks and data from attacks. It covers many different roles, from analysts who watch for threats to ethical hackers who test systems for weaknesses, so the first thing to know is that you do not have to learn everything at once.

Learn how computers and networks work

Before security, understand the basics: how Windows and Linux work, how the internet moves data (IP addresses, DNS, HTTP) and how networks are put together. Almost every security idea rests on these.

Learn the security basics

Study the core ideas of confidentiality, integrity and availability, the most common attacks (phishing, malware, password attacks and web vulnerabilities) and the basic defences (strong authentication, updates, firewalls and encryption).

Get hands-on, legally

Practise in environments built for learning, such as free capture-the-flag challenges, training labs, or a virtual machine on your own computer. Only test systems you own or have written permission to test. Anything else can be a crime.

Pick a direction

Common starting points are security analyst work (monitoring and responding to alerts), governance, risk and compliance (GRC), and penetration testing, also called ethical hacking, which usually needs a stronger technical base first.

Build proof of your skills

Keep write-ups of the labs and challenges you complete and consider an entry-level certification. Employers look for evidence of hands-on practice.
